# Sixto Rodriguez

* https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixto_Rodriguez


2026-11-08
Tom King lent me the DVD of Searching for the Sugar Man, probably in 2024 or early 2025. He said I must see it. Well, I'm not much of a movie fan, and it's taken me until November 8, 2026, to actually see the whole video.

But now I have watched it, watched the entire thing, perhaps the first and only movie I will see in 2026.

I think the first thing that I notice is that it's about Detroit. I spent a few days in Detroit visiting my daughter Cynthia when she was there on her internship. I recognize the city I saw in the movie The Dereliction the buildings that needed renovation. The abandonment of nice lives that broke Riga slid in.

During the movie, you never heard a whole song, but you heard snippets of multiple songs. It's hard to know about the music, but anyway, it did sound very much like Bob Dylan to me.

The songs are all about being, living rough, drugs, and being in a very working-class existence. And yet it turns out he got a degree in philosophy from Wayne State University. At the same time, he continued working in menial labor jobs. He talks working class, and yet he tries to run for office.

The movie makes him out to be unknown And that his tour of South Africa brings him back alive, But he had actually already toured Australia in '79 and '81. The movie embellishes the story. But you don't learn that from the movie. You only learn that if you look at the Wikipedia entry.

So it's a movie about somebody becoming famous and yet not becoming famous. So about being able to go on tour and going back and living in very humble circumstances. His three daughters seem very middle-class. His construction partner, who is also mentioned in the movie, also seems very well educated. So it's hard to know whether the rough incidents, drugs, prostitutes, and life in the street are actually a reality or just things that he can write songs about.

So there's a different between the life in the movie and the life in reality. The life in the songs and the life in reality.

The movie makes it seem that the movie the South Africans brought him out of obscurity and into fame, but in reality, he had already experienced some level of recognition through his earlier tours in Australia.

The interesting thing is he does not try to capitalize on his success. He continues to live in the house he bought for $50. He continues to work on humble jobs. While his daughters seemed to prosper.

So there may be interesting aspects to his life that He did not share. Why does he continue to live a humble life when he perhaps did not need to if he acted in different ways? So somehow he's sticking to his guns. He's sticking to the things that he wrote his songs about. He wanted nothing more, nothing less. It was interesting, and that's how he wanted to live.

Is he actually interesting? I'm not sure. I guess I would have to listen to the lyrics of his songs.

He is curiously authentic. Or perhaps totally non-authentic. He is awarded a degree in philosophy from Wayne State in 1981. In between two tours of Australia. Yet he continues to live in his crummy Detroit house and hang out in crummy Detroit neighborhoods.

An intentional enigma or a real enigma? Lives working class, but talks middle class.

I found the movie to be quite moving. But my level of appreciation was diminished after reading about his life on Wikipedia. The narrative in the movie was somewhat contrived and embellished. In other words, what happened in the movie was far greater than what may have happened in real life.

And if it had actually been as transformative an experience, then you would have thought that Rodriguez himself would express that, but he doesn't. He continues to be the person that he always was, somehow. If nothing else, the movie takes things from the late '60s all the way through the mid-2010s and compresses it into a much shorter-feeling time frame.
