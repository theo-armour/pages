# Structuralism

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structuralism

Structuralism is a 1949 approach to anthropology and the human sciences in general, derived from linguistics, that attempts to analyze a specific field as a complex system of interrelated parts.

Structuralism is an intellectual current and methodological approach, primarily in the social sciences, that interprets elements of human culture by way of their relationship to a broader system.[1] It works to uncover the structural patterns that underlie all things that humans do, think, perceive, and feel.

Alternatively, as summarized by philosopher Simon Blackburn, structuralism is:[2]

"The belief that phenomena of human life are not intelligible except through their interrelations. These relations constitute a structure, and behind local variations in the surface phenomena there are constant laws of abstract structure."

Phenomenology is a philosophical study and movement largely associated with the early 20th century that seeks to objectively investigate the nature of subjective, conscious experience.[1] It attempts to describe the universal features of consciousness while avoiding assumptions about the external world, aiming to describe phenomena as they appear, and to explore the meaning and significance of lived experience.

## 2026-09-07

I should keep a diary of a few lines after I read every section of The Frenchmen.

Emily is now moving us into deconstruction. It will be interesting to see if she's as skeptical about deconstruction as she is about structuralism.

I'm having issues with thinking about my own thinking. As we well know, I can only think of a few tiny bits at a time. Where my real thinking is really happening is when I'm writing, and I can write multiple senses that are tied together.

All of this relates back to my times of programming when I was in the zone and I could keep multiple lines, multiple paragraphs in my head at the same time.

I think this makes me feel that when Lacan or Derrida or any of these people are thinking about life in a philosophical manner, their brains are in the zone. They are remembering multiple lines of text and able to parse those as a complete thought in their brains, very similarly to what I was doing when I'm programming.

If this literary thinking, this can make for a wonderful read. If it is philosophical  writing, he can make a pile of mumbo jumbo.

So, what is deconstruction? Is it a careful parsing of the mumbo jumbo.


Well, if I write a bunch of good code, then another programmer should and can read my code and come to the same realization that I did. Therefore, you must assume that two people who create their own piles of mumbo jumbo can, by reading each other's mumbo jumbo, exchange mumbo jumbo, just the way I can exchange code with other programmers.

Is there a difference? Because my code and your code can run the program. And have the same output or different, as the case may be, but we know they are running.

Now, does mumbo jumbo have the same possibility? Can the mumbo jumbo become an operating system for life? If Christianity can become an operating system of life, why can Lacan's or Sartre's mumbo jumbo do the same?

If you and I both speak Python, we can understand each other quite quickly, but if you are Python and I am JavaScript, we can't really communicate.

The programs we make might do exactly the same thing. Have identical functionality Nonetheless, the two of us might have difficulty talking about them because of our different programming backgrounds.

And so now we can really differentiate between what the program does, What function does it carries out, and what is making it run, And the tools used to make it run

We have the result, the application that makes the result, and then the design of the application. Each can have its own observers. And each can be reduced to NPCs/objects by other observers.

Basically, all ends up being horrendously complicated, almost all at once. Much easier to forget about it all and just live in the moment.
